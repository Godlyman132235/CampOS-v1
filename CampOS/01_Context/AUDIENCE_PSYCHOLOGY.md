The below is a perspective on how I expect the target audience to feel and think when it comes to vibe coding and their use of AI agents. Use this as a strong evidence backbone towards the project, which I'm making, which is CAMPOS v1. V1, meaning version 1, as this is only a prototype which I will be posting online for validation before I make the production-ready version:

Just to give you context on the audience themselves, I generally expect them to be in the age range of 16 to 24, with the gender of male and the identification as a vibe coder. Now, a vibe coder is someone that uses AI completely to code without actually using their mind in the process itself. This is very limiting, as coders will then feel that they do not understand what they are doing, leading them to confusion and frustration towards what they are trying to build.

This then often leads the vibe coder to do two things:

1. Continue their messy vibe coding ways, trying to improve everything they can.
2. At the same time, bear the cost of being confused and frustrated every time something goes wrong, as they do not understand how to fix it. Or they have an aha moment where they realize that they only need to tweak a few things in order for them to be in control rather than just the AI.

I've had one of these aha moments before, when I first started my vibe coding journey about four months ago. I used to only do chatting with the agent by itself without planning. I would just keep describing what changes I wanted, going back and forth, checking what I wanted to change, what I wanted to do next, and also how I wanted to improve things. This, of course, because it is limited in context as well as limited in information, led to this gradual and slow decline of my energy, as well as the agent's energy, leading the project to then only get worse and worse. This was so until I heard the term agentic engineering and its five pillars. It finally gave me an understanding of the one thing which I was missing, which was control over my own project rather than the AI. This moment has forever changed my journey and now has allowed me to freely do what I want rather than the agent slowing down over time.

Now, with the term agentic engineering stuck in my mind (Thanks to John Kim), I can now finally do things for myself rather than the AI doing it. This is not only giving my productivity a boost but also increasing my intrinsic motivation, because now I understand how to control the agent rather than the agent controlling me and my work. I want more of these people to have an aha moment. For that reason, I'm making CAMPOS because I have too felt this, and I want to make everyone else's experience a lot easier than mine as of right now.
